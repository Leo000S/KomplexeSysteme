\section*{11.1: Entanglement of a pure 2-qubit state}
\subsection*{(a)}

We calculated the matrix and verified it's purity (the trace of it's square has to be one) in python with this code:

\begin{minted}{python}
import sympy as sp
i = sp.I
sqrt2 = sp.sqrt(2)

psi = sp.Matrix([
    sp.Rational(1, 5),
    (2 * i * sqrt2) / 5,
    sp.Rational(-4, 15),
    (-8 * i * sqrt2) / 15])

rho = psi * psi.H

print("latex code")
print(sp.latex(rho))

rho_squared = rho**2
purity = rho_squared.trace()

print("purity")
print(f"Tr(rho²) = {purity}")
\end{minted}

\begin{align}
\rho=

    \frac{2 \sqrt{2} i}{25} & \frac{8}{25} & - \frac{8 \sqrt{2} i}{75} & - \frac{32}{75}\\[0.1cm]
    - \frac{4}{75} & \frac{8 \sqrt{2} i}{75} & \frac{16}{225} & - \frac{32 \sqrt{2} i}{225}\\[0.1cm]
    - \frac{8 \sqrt{2} i}{75} & - \frac{32}{75} & \frac{32 \sqrt{2} i}{225} & \frac{128}{225}

We calculated the reduced matrices with the by carrying out the partial traces in python:
\begin{minted}{python}
import sympy as sp

i = sp.I
sqrt2 = sp.sqrt(2)

    sp.Rational(1, 5),
    (2 * i * sqrt2) / 5,

rho = psi * psi.H

rho_L = sp.Matrix([

rho_R = sp.Matrix([
    [rho[0,0] + rho[2,2], rho[0,1] + rho[2,3]],
    [rho[1,0] + rho[3,2], rho[1,1] + rho[3,3]]

print("rho_L")
print(sp.latex(rho_L))
print("\n rho_R")
print(sp.latex(rho_R))
\end{minted}

\subsection*{(c)}
A pure state is called entangled if the reduced density matrices are mixed. To check if $\rho$ is entangle we simply check, if the reduced density matrices are pure.
With Python (code similar to the code above) we calculated:
\begin{align}
        \text{Tr}(\rho_L^2)=1
        \text{Tr}(\rho_R^2)=1
\end{align}
This means, $\rho$ is not entangled.

\section*{11.2: Quantum Cryptography}
\subsection*{(a)}
We distinguish the four possible combinations of Alice's preparation and Bob's measurement:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Case $a=0$, $b=0$:} Alice sends $|0\rangle$ and Bob measures with $M_0$. Since
    \[
    M_0|0\rangle = |0\rangle,
    \]
    the outcome is always $m=+1$. Hence, $m=-1$ cannot occur.

    \item \textbf{Case $a=0$, $b=1$:} Alice sends $|0\rangle$ and Bob measures with $M_1$. Using
    \[
    |0\rangle=\frac{|+\rangle+|-\rangle}{\sqrt{2}},
    \]
    Bob obtains $m=-1$ with probability $1/2$.

    \item \textbf{Case $a=1$, $b=0$:} Alice sends $|+\rangle$ and Bob measures with $M_0$. Since
    \[
    |+\rangle=\frac{|0\rangle+|1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}},
    \]
    the outcome $m=-1$ occurs with probability $1/2$.

    \item \textbf{Case $a=1$, $b=1$:} Alice sends $|+\rangle$ and Bob measures with $M_1$. Since
    \[
    M_1|+\rangle = |+\rangle,
    \]
    the outcome is always $m=+1$, so $m=-1$ cannot occur.
\end{itemize}

Therefore, the outcome $m=-1$ is only possible for the two cases
\[
(a,b)=(0,1)\quad\text{and}\quad(a,b)=(1,0),
\]
which implies
\[
\boxed{a=1-b.}
\]
\subsection*{(b)}
A key bit is obtained only in rounds where Bob obtains the outcome $m=-1$,
since only then Alice and Bob can conclude from part (a) that $a=1-b$.
We compute the success probability by using the cases from part (a).

Each pair $(a,b)$ occurs with probability $1/4$. From part (a), we know that
\[
P(m=-1 \mid a=0,b=0)=0,\qquad P(m=-1 \mid a=1,b=1)=0,
\]
while
\[
P(m=-1 \mid a=0,b=1)=\frac{1}{2},\qquad P(m=-1 \mid a=1,b=0)=\frac{1}{2}.
\]

Hence, the total success probability is
\[
P(m=-1)=\frac{1}{4}\cdot 0 + \frac{1}{4}\cdot \frac{1}{2}
+ \frac{1}{4}\cdot \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4}\cdot 0
= \frac{1}{4}.
\]

Since each transmission uses one qubit,
the average number of qubits needed to obtain one successful key bit is the
inverse of the success probability:
\[
\boxed{\langle N \rangle = \frac{1}{P(m=-1)} = 4.}
\]

\subsection*{(c)}
Eve performs a measurement in the orthonormal basis
\[
|\phi_+\rangle=\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle,\qquad
|\phi_-\rangle=\beta^*|0\rangle-\alpha^*|1\rangle,
\]
with $|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2=1$. After Eve's measurement, the state collapses to
$|\phi_\pm\rangle$ and is measured by Bob. The resulting
probabilities are obtained with the following calculations.

Consider the case where Alice sends $|0\rangle$ and Bob measures
with $M_0$. The probability of obtaining the outcome $m=-1$ is
\[
\begin{aligned}
P(m=-1)
&=P(\phi_+)P(m=-1|\phi_+) + P(\phi_-)P(m=-1|\phi_-)\\
&=|\langle\phi_+|0\rangle|^2\,|\langle1|\phi_+\rangle|^2
 +|\langle\phi_-|0\rangle|^2\,|\langle1|\phi_-\rangle|^2\\
&=|\alpha|^2|\beta|^2+|\beta|^2|\alpha|^2\\
&=2|\alpha|^2(1-|\alpha|^2).
\end{aligned}
\]

Introducing
\[
u:=|\alpha|^2,
\]
this becomes
\[
P(m=-1)=2u(1-u).
\]

For the second relevant case, where Alice sends $|0\rangle$ and Bob measures
with $M_1$, one obtains
\[
\begin{aligned}
P(m=-1)
&=|\langle\phi_+|0\rangle|^2\,|\langle-|\phi_+\rangle|^2
 +|\langle\phi_-|0\rangle|^2\,|\langle-|\phi_-\rangle|^2\\
&=\frac12|\alpha|^2|\beta-\alpha|^2
+\frac12|\beta|^2|\beta+\alpha|^2\\
\end{aligned}
\]

Defining
\[
v:=\frac12|\alpha+\beta|^2,
\]
and using $ \frac12 |\alpha-\beta|^2=1-v$ we obtain
\[
P(m=-1)= u (1-v) + v (1-u) = v + u - 2 u v.
\]

The remaining two cases are due to analogous derivations
\[
P(m=-1\,|\,a=1,b=0)=v + u - 2 u v,\qquad
P(m=-1\,|\,a=1,b=1)=2u(1-u).
\]

We see that Eve modifies Bob's measurement statistics, which depend
only on two parameters
\[
\boxed{u=|\alpha|^2,\qquad
v=\frac12|\alpha+\beta|^2.}
\]

\subsection*{(d)}
Alice and Bob will not recognize the intervention of Eve, if the statistics stay the same.
To obtain the same statistic, we have two conditions for the probabilities:
\[
P(m=-1\,|\,a=b)= v + u - 2 u v = 0.5,\qquad
P(m=-1\,|\,a\ne b)=2u(1-u) = 0.
\]
This implies, that u is $u=0$ or $u=1$. For the two cases, we obtain
\begin{itemize}
    \item $u=0$: $P(m=-1\,|\,a=b)= v + 0 - 2 0 v = 0.5 \implies v = 0.5$
    \item $u=1$: $P(m=-1\,|\,a=b)= v + 1 - 2 v = 1-v = 0.5 \implies v = 0.5$
\end{itemize}
For both cases, Eve will measure in the $|0\rangle |1\rangle$ basis.
\subsection*{(e)}
In the case of (d), Eve can not detect the $|+\rangle$ probably. When she measures this state, it will colapse in $|0\rangle$ or $|1\rangle$.
She wont get well defined result due to the different probabilities.
This means, that Eve will lose about $50\%$ of the keys information.
